Biography

Nicholas Van-Orton is an independent researcher and artist working at the intersection of visual theory, color studies, and algorithmic culture.


His research addresses the need for coherent theoretical frameworks capable of accounting for color and visual systems under contemporary computational conditions.


His work is primarily theoretical and operates across art history, aesthetics, and digital humanities, with a particular focus on color as a structural and autonomous component of visual organization.


He examines how historical models of color theory, especially within modernism, can be reinterpreted in relation to generative and algorithmic visual systems.


In parallel with his research practice, he develops and curates independent scholarly infrastructures and research communities, contributing to open, transdisciplinary forms of knowledge production beyond traditional institutional frameworks.


His current work focuses on the development of conceptual tools for understanding visual perception, color systems, and visual epistemology in the age of algorithms.
